0 · The one-paragraph version

Each pore body $i$ becomes a node; each throat $k$ between bodies $i$ and $j$ becomes a chain of three resistors (body-$i$ half-link, throat, body-$j$ half-link). The half-link from body $i$ to throat $k$ is modelled as a prism of volume $V_{ik}=V_i/z_i$ and length $d_{ik}$, so its conductance is

$$G_{ik}=\frac{\sigma A_{ik}}{d_{ik}}=\frac{\sigma V_{ik}}{d_{ik}^{2}}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad d\to\lambda d\ \text{at fixed }V\ \Rightarrow\ G\to G/\lambda^{2}.$$

The vanilla RN takes $d_{ik}=\lVert\mathbf p_k-\mathbf c_i\rVert$ from the body centroid, so current that enters body $i$ through throat $p$ and leaves through throat $r$ is charged the route through the centroid, $d_{ip}+d_{ir}$. The shortest possible route is the straight chord $\lVert\mathbf p_p-\mathbf p_r\rVert$. The short-circuit RN keeps the centroid where it is and only shortens each half-link along its own ray, so that every pair of throats ends up between those two limits:

$$\lVert\mathbf p_p-\mathbf p_r\rVert\ \le\ \tilde d_{ip}+\tilde d_{ir}\ \le\ d_{ip}+d_{ir}\qquad\text{for every pair of throats }(p,r)\text{ of every interior body.}$$

The solve does not know in advance which throats are inflow and which are outflow, so the lengths are the least-resistance ones that satisfy all pairs at once. A body with two throats gets exactly its chord. A body with more throats gets lengths above the chords and below the centroid route. All volumes $V_{ik}$ are unchanged.

1 · Code walkthrough: what each stage does, in the order the code runs it

Stages marked same are identical for both RN variants; only stage 3 differs. Function names link to section 7 (file and line).

Stage 1 · Clean the watershed labels: two merge passes same

Code: merge_boundary_only_bodies_iterative → merge_short_circuit_bodies, called inside extract_throats_from_bodies_voxel_parallel(merge_bodies=True). Both run on _LabelGraph, the 6-connected adjacency graph of the label array (two bodies are neighbours iff they share a voxel face).

1A · Boundary-only face bodies

A face body touches the inlet ($x=0$) or outlet ($x=L_x-1$) face. If all of its neighbours are face bodies too, it cannot feed current into the interior on its own:

$$\mathcal N(i)\subseteq\mathcal F\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{merge } i \text{ into } \arg\max_{j\in\mathcal N(i)\cap\mathcal F} V_j$$

Repeated until no such body is left (a merge can expose another). "Largest" is by voxel count $V_j$, not by contact area.

1B · Interior bodies that only touch boundary bodies

An interior body whose every neighbour is an inlet/outlet body is either a dead end on one face or a short circuit between same-potential (or inlet-to-outlet) nodes. Either way it is folded into its largest boundary neighbour:

$$\mathcal N(i)\neq\varnothing,\ \ \mathcal N(i)\subseteq\mathcal F\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{merge } i \text{ into } \arg\max_{j\in\mathcal N(i)} V_j$$

Also iterated to convergence. Merges never move voxels between the pore and the solid, so total pore volume is unchanged.

Why this matters for the slice plots (5.3)Merged-away labels no longer exist in the network. Anything that looks up a potential by the raw watershed label finds nothing for them; that is what painted parts of inlet bodies at 0 V in the previous version of this page (see 5.3).

Stage 2 · Body properties and throat extraction same

Code: compute_initial_body_props, find_candidate_pairs, _extract_one_pair, compute_throat_areas, estimate_plane_normal, get_planar_area, _assemble_network.

Bodies

$$V_i=\lvert\Omega_i\rvert,\qquad \mathbf c_i^{\mathrm{vol}}=\frac1{V_i}\sum_{\mathbf x\in\Omega_i}\mathbf x$$

A body touching either flow face is a surface body; its RN node is then the centroid of its face voxels,

$$\mathbf c_i=\frac{1}{\lvert\Omega_i\cap\partial\Omega_x\rvert}\sum_{\mathbf x\in\Omega_i\cap\partial\Omega_x}\mathbf x\quad\text{(surface bodies)},\qquad \mathbf c_i=\mathbf c_i^{\mathrm{vol}}\ \text{otherwise}.$$

The solver classifies a surface body as inlet if $c_{i,x}\le1$ and outlet if $c_{i,x}\ge L_x-2$ (get_inlet_outlet_indices).

Throats

Candidate pairs come from overlapping padded bounding boxes (surface–surface pairs skipped). For a pair $(i,j)$, with $\mathcal D$ the 6-connected dilation and $\partial^{\rm in}$ the inner label boundaries:

$$T_k=\mathcal D(\Omega_i)\cap\mathcal D(\Omega_j)\cap\partial^{\rm in}\cap\Omega_{\rm pore},\qquad \lvert T_k\rvert\ge n_{\min}=1$$
$$\mathbf p_k=\frac1{\lvert T_k\rvert}\sum_{\mathbf x\in T_k}\mathbf x,\qquad \hat{\mathbf n}_k=\text{smallest right-singular vector of }\{\mathbf x-\mathbf p_k\}$$
$$A_{t,k}=\operatorname{area}\Big(\bigcup_{\mathbf x\in T_k}\operatorname{hull}\big(\Pi_{\hat{\mathbf n}_k}(\text{8 corners of voxel }\mathbf x)\big)\Big)$$

vox_projection: each throat voxel is projected onto the best-fit plane, and the union of the projected voxel footprints is measured. That handles non-convex and stair-cased throats.

Stage 3 · Half-link volume, length and area: vanilla vs short-circuit

Code: _compute_volume_share, compute_short_circuit_distances (short-circuit only), _compute_body_side(d_override), selected by rn_method in calculate_network_effective_properties.

$$V_{ik}=\frac{V_i}{z_i}\ \ (\texttt{split\_volume\_equal=True}),\qquad A_{ik}=\frac{V_{ik}}{d_{ik}}\qquad\text{identical volumes in both variants}$$
vanilla
$$d_{ik}=\lVert\mathbf p_k-\mathbf c_i\rVert$$

Current entering body $i$ at throat $p$ and leaving at throat $r$ is charged the star length $d_{ip}+d_{ir}\ge\lVert\mathbf p_p-\mathbf p_r\rVert$. For a surface body $\mathbf c_i$ is the face-patch centroid, which adds a transverse detour along the equipotential face.

short-circuit

The centroid $\mathbf c_i$ stays; each half-link is shortened along its own ray, $\tilde d_{ik}\le d_{ik}$.

Interior bodies ($z_i\ge2$): the least-resistance lengths that keep every throat pair at or above its chord,

$$\min_{\tilde d}\ \sum_k\frac{\tilde d_{ik}^{\,2}}{\sigma V_{ik}}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \tilde d_{ip}+\tilde d_{ir}\ge\lVert\mathbf p_p-\mathbf p_r\rVert\ \ \forall\,p\ne r,\qquad \min(0.5,d_{ik})\le\tilde d_{ik}\le d_{ik}$$

The objective is the body's own half-link resistance (each $R_{ik}=\tilde d_{ik}/(\sigma A_{ik})=\tilde d_{ik}^2/(\sigma V_{ik})$). The vanilla lengths always satisfy the constraints (triangle inequality through $\mathbf c_i$), so a solution exists. For $z_i=2$ the answer is the chord itself, split so the body behaves as one uniform straight tube of its volume between the two throats. Solved per body (closed form for $z_i=2$, SLSQP otherwise), then made exactly feasible by moving back towards $d$ just far enough.

Inlet/outlet bodies are Dirichlet nodes on the face, so a throat is reached straight from that equipotential face:

$$\tilde d_{ik}=\lvert p_{k,x}-c_{i,x}\rvert\ \le d_{ik}$$

The perpendicular distance from the face plane through the node, which is the shortest path from the face to throat $k$. Dead-end bodies ($z_i=1$) have no pair and stay vanilla.

Both bounds hold by constructionUpper bound: $\tilde d_{ik}\le d_{ik}$ for every half-link, so no path through a body is longer than vanilla's route through the centroid. Lower bound: $\tilde d_{ip}+\tilde d_{ir}\ge\lVert\mathbf p_p-\mathbf p_r\rVert$ is a constraint of the optimisation for every pair of throats, so no path is shorter than the straight line between its throats. Pairs through an inlet/outlet node are not bounded by the chord, because current there passes through the equipotential electrode.

Stage 4 · Berg's three-segment geometry and the uniform-pipe fallback same rules

Code: _compute_body_side, _compute_fallback, _store_body_connection, _store_throat_properties; then build_network_arrays and calculate_conductances in berg_cc.py. The rules are identical; the inputs $d_{ik},A_{ik}$ come from stage 3.

Case A · converging link ($r_1\le1$ and $r_2\le1$)

With $r_i=A_{t,k}/A_{ik}$ and $\alpha=0.5$, each side splits into a body segment and half of the throat segment:

$$d^{\rm eff}_{ik}=d_{ik}\big(1-\alpha\sqrt{r_i}\big),\qquad A^{\rm eff}_{ik}=A_{ik}\,\frac{1-\alpha r_i^{3/2}}{1-\alpha\sqrt{r_i}},\qquad \ell_{t,i}=d_{ik}-d^{\rm eff}_{ik}=\alpha\sqrt{r_i}\,d_{ik}$$

The throat segment has area $A_{t,k}$ and length $\ell_{t,1}+\ell_{t,2}$. Volume is conserved on each side exactly:

$$A^{\rm eff}_{ik}d^{\rm eff}_{ik}+A_{t,k}\,\ell_{t,i}=A_{ik}d_{ik}=V_{ik}.$$

Case B · fallback ($r_1>1$ or $r_2>1$)

The throat is wider than a body's mean cross-section, so a converging segment cannot be built. The whole link becomes one uniform pipe, cut into three equal segments:

$$A_u=\frac{V_{1k}+V_{2k}}{d_{1k}+d_{2k}},\qquad \ell_1=\ell_t=\ell_2=\frac{d_{1k}+d_{2k}}{3},\qquad \text{all at area }A_u$$

Volume and total length are conserved, and the area is the same on both sides of the throat (flux continuity). Note that the constriction at that throat disappears from the model.

$$\frac1{G_k}=R_1+R_t+R_2=\frac{\ell_1}{\sigma A_1}+\frac{\ell_t}{\sigma A_t}+\frac{\ell_2}{\sigma A_2}\qquad(\sigma=1,\ \text{voxel units})$$

Stage 5 · Kirchhoff solve same

Code: get_inlet_outlet_indices, solve_potential_field (sparse LU via spsolve).

$$\sum_{k=(i,j)}G_k\,(\Phi_i-\Phi_j)=0\ \ \text{(interior)},\qquad \Phi_i=\Delta V=1\ \text{(inlet)},\qquad \Phi_i=0\ \text{(outlet)}$$

Isolated nodes (no throat) are pinned to 0 so the matrix stays non-singular; they carry no current. The throat currents and the network conductance follow directly:

$$I_k=G_k(\Phi_i-\Phi_j),\qquad I_{\rm tot}=\sum_{k\,\ni\,\text{inlet}}\lvert I_k\rvert,\qquad \frac1F\Big|_{G}=\frac{I_{\rm tot}\,\Delta s}{A_\perp\,\Delta V}$$

Stage 6 · Streamtubes and Berg's $1/F$, $\tau_c$, $C_c$ same

Code: build_streamtubes (flow_decomposition), recover_segment_potentials, compute_local_iota_squared, compute_streamtube_properties, compute_all_berg.

Local field and $1/F$

$$\Phi_{1t}=\Phi_i-I_kR_1,\qquad \Phi_{2t}=\Phi_j+I_kR_2$$
$$\iota^2_{s}=\Big(\frac{\Delta\Phi_s}{\ell_s}\Big)^2\Big(\frac{\Delta s}{\Delta V}\Big)^2,\qquad \iota^2_g=\frac{1}{\Omega}\sum_s V_s\,\iota^2_s,\qquad \frac1F=\iota^2_g\,\varphi,\ \ \varphi=\frac{\Omega}{V_{\rm box}}$$

$s$ runs over all three segments of every throat, and $\Omega=\sum_s V_s$ is the network pore volume. By the power identity, this equals the $1/F$ from $I_{\rm tot}$ in stage 5.

Streamtubes

The current field is split greedily into source-to-sink paths $\Gamma$ carrying $I_\Gamma$, with

$$V_\Gamma=\sum_{k\in\Gamma}\frac{I_\Gamma}{\lvert I_k\rvert}V_k,\qquad L_\Gamma=\sum_{k\in\Gamma}(\ell_1+\ell_t+\ell_2)_k=\sum_{k\in\Gamma}(d_{1k}+d_{2k})$$
$$\tau(\Gamma)=\frac{\Delta s}{L_\Gamma},\qquad C(\Gamma)=\frac{\Delta V}{L_\Gamma^2}\sum_{s\in\Gamma}\frac{\ell_s^2}{\lvert\Delta\Phi_s\rvert}$$
$$\tau_c^2=\frac{1}{\Omega_c}\sum_\Gamma V_\Gamma\,\tau(\Gamma)^2,\qquad C_c=\frac{1}{I_{\rm tot}}\sum_\Gamma I_\Gamma\,C(\Gamma),\qquad \boxed{\ \frac1F=\varphi_c\,\frac{\tau_c^2}{C_c}\ }\quad(\text{Berg 2012, Eq. 17, exact on the network})$$

The page reports the tortuosity as $\tau_{c,B}=(\tau_c^2)^{-1/2}\ge1$ and the constrictivity as $1/C_c$. It also reports the current-weighted tortuosity

$$T_I=\frac{\sum_\Gamma I_\Gamma L_\Gamma}{I_{\rm tot}\,\Delta s}\qquad\text{the network analogue of TauFactor's}\quad \tau^{\rm TF}=\frac{\langle\lvert\mathbf J\rvert\rangle}{\lvert\langle J_x\rangle\rvert},$$

because $\int\lvert\mathbf J\rvert\,dV=\sum_\Gamma I_\Gamma L_\Gamma$ and $\int J_x\,dV=\sum_\Gamma I_\Gamma\Delta s$. Berg's $\tau_{c,B}$ is a volume-weighted average of $(\Delta s/L_\Gamma)^2$ and is always somewhat larger than $T_I$, so compare $T_I$ with TauFactor.

3.1 · before the solve Geometry: where each half-link starts and ends

Every half-link of the body runs from a throat centroid $\mathbf p_k$ towards the body centroid $\mathbf c_i$. Vanilla runs the full distance $d_{ik}$. The short-circuit keeps the same ray but stops after $\tilde d_{ik}\le d_{ik}$ (solid), and the faint remainder is the detour it removes. The lengths are chosen so that each of the six throat pairs is charged at least its chord and at most its vanilla route. The table under the drawings lists every pair.

3.2 · before the solve The resistor circuit the code builds from it

Each throat becomes three resistors in series: the body's own half-link $R_b$ (from its centre to the throat), the throat segment $R_t$, and the neighbour's half-link $R_n$. Their sum is the link resistance $1/G_k$. The body's potential $\Phi_i$ and its neighbours' are still unknown. An amber link is a uniform-pipe fallback, where the three resistors are equal and the constriction is not represented.

4 · Network-wide distributions: what each histogram measures and what it tells us

All distributions are over the whole network of the sample. Set how a histogram is normalised:

How to read the three normalisationsCounts: number of elements per bin; the two variants have the same elements, so their counts are directly comparable. Probability density: counts divided by $N\,\Delta b$, so the area under each curve is 1 and only the shape is compared. CDF: the fraction of elements below a value, where medians ($0.5$) and tails are easiest to read. Histograms marked "current-weighted" weight each element by the current it carries ($\sum w=1$), so they show where the current sits, not where the geometry sits.

Summary statistics

$\bar x$ = mean, $\tilde x$ = median, $P_{10}/P_{90}$ = 10th/90th percentiles, IQR = $P_{75}-P_{25}$ (spread that ignores outliers). The current-weighted mean is $\sum_e w_e x_e/\sum_e w_e$.

5 · Against the TauFactor voxel solution of the same sample

TauFactor solves Laplace's equation on every pore voxel with unit conductance per voxel face, $\Phi=1$ at the inlet and $0$ at the outlet. To compare at the network scale:

$$\bar\phi_i=\frac1{V_i}\sum_{\mathbf x\in\Omega_i}\phi(\mathbf x),\qquad I^{\rm TF}_k=\sum_{\substack{\text{faces }(u,v)\\ u\in\Omega_i,\ v\in\Omega_j}}(\phi_u-\phi_v),\qquad I^{\rm TF}_{\rm tot}=\sum_{\text{faces across a plane }x=\text{const}}(\phi_u-\phi_v)$$

Normalised currents $I_k/I_{\rm tot}$ (each method by its own total) compare where the current goes, independent of the overall $1/F$ level. Raw currents compare magnitudes, so vanilla's lower $1/F$ shows up as a slope below 1.

6 · Benchmark on the 10 Fine microstructures (data_new)

Same configuration as this page, run by rn_testbench/run_variants.sh; TauFactor from data_new/all_runs_versions.csv.

$1/F_{\rm RN}$ vs TauFactor $D_{\rm eff}$

Log–log. One marker per sample. Lines join the two variants of one sample.

Exact error budget (geometric means over the 10 samples)

Using the network identity $1/F=\varphi_c\tau_c^2/C_c$ and TauFactor's definition $D=\varepsilon C^{\rm TF}/(\tau^{\rm TF})^2$:

$$\frac{1/F_{\rm RN}}{D^{\rm TF}}=\underbrace{\frac{\varphi_c}{\varepsilon}}_{\text{porosity}}\times\underbrace{\frac{1/C_c}{C^{\rm TF}}}_{\text{constriction}}\times\underbrace{\Big(\frac{\tau^{\rm TF}}{\tau_{c,B}}\Big)^{2}}_{\text{tortuosity}}$$
What the short-circuit does on Fine mediaGeometric means over the 10 samples: $1/F$ goes from $0.618$ (vanilla) to $0.731$ of TauFactor, and the $T_I$ error from $+24\%$ to $+12\%$. The porosity and constriction factors stay within $5\%$ of 1, so almost all of the remaining error is still the tortuosity factor $(\tau^{\rm TF}/\tau_{c,B})^2$. An earlier rule (git history) reached $0.93$, but only by charging $47\%$ of throat pairs less than their chord. The present rule never does.
